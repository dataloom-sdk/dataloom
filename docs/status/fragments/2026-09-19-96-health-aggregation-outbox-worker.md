# Fragment: `#96` health aggregation of outbox and queue-worker state (2026-09-19)

For the lead to fold into `docs/status/market-readiness.md`. Not edited by the PR itself.

## (a) Proposed "Recently shipped" row

| 2026-09-19 | Decision D4: `dataLoomHealthSnapshot` now aggregates the two subsystems it previously excluded because neither exposed a synchronous read, and rolls everything up into a configurable `HEALTHY`/`DEGRADED`/`UNHEALTHY` severity. **Outbox (a pushed cache, deliberately not a store read):** `DurableOperationalEventOutbox` takes an optional `stateObserver` and pushes a bounded, payload-free `OperationalEventOutboxStateObservation` (pending count, oldest pending `occurredAt`, retained-acknowledged count, store version, `observedAt`) after every state it loads-and-evaluates or successfully writes (reads, idempotent `append`, no-op `acknowledge`, every successful compare-and-set; nothing for failed loads, lost races or persistence failures; observer exceptions swallowed; with no observer nothing is computed and no clock is read). `OperationalEventOutboxHealthTracker` keeps the latest per scope, merged by store version, and `DurableOperationalEventOutboxProcessor(outbox, tracker)` also reports each cycle's outcome and skipped/failed/acknowledge-failed counts (the outbox cannot know what a handler decided). The guarantee is stated in the KDoc and `docs/api/health-snapshot.md`: a value is what *this process's outbox instance last saw or wrote*, not the store's current state; the snapshot reports `observedAt`, `observationAge` and a `stale` flag (default 10 minutes) instead of presenting the cache as authoritative, and a stale observation that had pending entries is `DEGRADED`. **Queue worker:** `QueueWorkerHealthTracker`, fed by `DataLoomQueueWorker.withHealthTracking` / `DataLoomCircuitQueueWorker.withHealthTracking` wrappers (result and exceptions pass through unchanged), records exact bookkeeping for runs made through the wrapper: runs in flight, last start, last normal completion, last empty-queue run, a closed `QueueWorkerRunOutcome`, consecutive failed runs, and the last failure's closed error vocabulary only (`DataLoomError.message`/`cause` are never read); cancellation is neither a failure nor an outcome. **Roll-up:** `DataLoomHealthSnapshot` gains `outboxHealth`, `queueWorkerHealth`, `severity` and `findings` (closed-enum `component`/`code`/`severity` plus a non-sensitive subject, no free text); thresholds in `DataLoomHealthThresholds` (outbox pending 1,000/5,000, oldest pending 1 h/24 h, observation stale 10 min, worker failed runs 1/3, stuck run 30 min; all validated). Provider health and telemetry-exporter health now roll up too; `providerLifecycleState` is reported but deliberately does not affect severity (a phase, not a verdict), and a snapshot built with nothing is `HEALTHY` with no findings (documented as "no evidence of a problem", not "all checked"). `now` is a required argument whenever observations are supplied (the function still reads no clock). **Opt-in wiring:** `DataLoomBuilder.operationalEventOutboxHealthTracker(tracker)` (observer on all five builder-constructed outboxes) and `DataLoomBuilder.queueWorkerHealthTracker(tracker)` (wraps the built direct or circuit worker); unset, every outbox and worker is exactly as before. **Verified:** `:dataloom-api:jvmTest` (980 tests, 0 failures) and `:dataloom-runtime:jvmTest` (1,881 tests, 0 failures) on the Windows JVM, including 11 new outbox-observation tests (bounded summary at the right version, no observer means no clock read, lost race reports only the winning retry, throwing observer never fails a call, observation is a snapshot not a live view), 18 table-driven roll-up tests (every default threshold at, just under and past its boundary with exact finding codes; stale/fresh/never-observed; last-cycle indicator; worker failures, stuck runs, activity, informational staleness; provider/exporter mapping; custom and invalid thresholds; absence-is-inert), 18 tracker integration tests (real outbox and processor over an in-memory store, including proof that a second instance's writes are invisible until the observing outbox touches the store; version-ordered merge; in-flight/idle/cancelled/exception/concurrent runs for both worker types) and 2 builder tests through a real `DataLoomBuilder`-built worker (both trackers wired: outbox bridged entry and worker run observed; neither wired: nothing observed and the result unchanged). `checkKotlinAbi -Pdataloom.appleKlibCrossCompile=true` passes whole-build (JVM `.api` + iOS `.klib.api` regenerated for `dataloom-api` and `dataloom-runtime`; removed lines are only the intentionally re-shaped `DataLoomHealthSnapshot`, `dataLoomHealthSnapshot`, processor and outbox constructors); `compileKotlinIosSimulatorArm64` and `compileTestKotlinIosSimulatorArm64` pass for both modules. Neither module has an Android target, so there is no Android compile to run. **Not verified:** Kotlin/Native (iOS Simulator) execution of the new tests (cross-compiled only; no wall-clock dependency); a builder-level test for the circuit-aware worker wrapper (the wrapper itself is covered directly); a real shared-store multi-process scenario (the staleness behaviour is proven with two outbox instances over one in-memory store); the default thresholds are reasoned, not tuned from production data. See `docs/api/health-snapshot.md` (`#96`, 2026-09-19) | `#96` feature |

## (b) Row percentage

`#96`: 55% -> **unchanged** proposed, or at most 60% if the lead judges the health/read-model gap closed enough. Justification for holding: the "SDK-wide health aggregation" gap is narrowed (outbox and queue worker now aggregate, with a roll-up) but not closed — assets, configuration/policy history, scheduler and the plugin engine still contribute nothing to `severity`, and the deployable operations dashboard/adaptor is untouched.
